I have been drifting back to my usual reading habits these days. I finished reading ***My Brilliant Friend*** by Elena Ferrante within three days. I like books that have an authentic feeling to them. And just like how Lila and Elena are deeply affected by their upbringing in Naples, I feel like I still carry a large part of Saigon and those chaotic childhood and teenage years in me. The ending of the first book was a bit too unnatural for my liking. When the author showed their manipulative hand in the characters’ lives, the story suddenly lost its magic. Then I DNF’d the second book in the quartet at 30%. The symbolism and the structural changes in the narrative were too much for me to not think of this book as a melodrama. Another factor was that I could see where the story was going with Nino, Elena, and Lila. The questions remain: should or would a person in a deeply disadvantaged situation let their childhood traumas overcome their rational brain, or use their wits to find the best path out? Some may say that feelings are not that easy to ignore, and that what Lila did was a rebellion. After reading a lot of the same tragedies in fiction and watching those tragedies play out in real life, I just find this whole tragedy repetitive. And what kind of rebellion is that if it relies on a man who isn’t even worth it?
<!--more-->
I also watched ***Children of Men***. Neat little story - the biblical parallels were deeply ingrained in the film. The scene where they carry the baby through the fighting soldiers, causing a brief ceasefire before the soldiers resume shooting at each other again, was one of the most brilliant scenes I have ever watched. I also watched ***Lady Bird***. I kept thinking about how good college PR was during that period of time - maybe it still is? I’m not sure.

I read ***The Seven Year Slip*** by Ashley Poston, a cute little story. Not sure if I would have the patience if someone kept ghosting me again and again like that. But hey, you do you, Iwan. I also finished the first book of the ***Murderbot*** series. The book was okay, a little bit slow-paced for my liking, but the ending saved it. Now I’m waiting for the next book in the series to become available through Libby so I can continue. I have also been reading ***The Years*** by Annie Ernaux. Brilliant writer - very sharp, witty, and funny. The English translation, on the other hand, leaves a lot to be desired. I feel like readers would have been able to appreciate this book more if there were tons of footnotes; I wonder why they chose to include so few. With my limited knowledge of French history and culture, I decided to stop reading at 35%. Maybe I will revisit this book when I have read more or when there’s a better edition out there. Still, I love the structure of this book - the way the author's personal life details are intertwined with her sharp observations on how history and culture affect individual lives.

The ***Watchmen*** comic book was amazing. The plot twist was expected, but the story was so good and logical that there was nothing to complain about regarding the flow. I’m slightly disappointed (well, maybe not slightly) at how they treated the female characters in this book; the whole thing between Sally and the Comedian was uncalled for. And Laurel reminds me of Julia in ***1984*** - their roles made me question why the authors limited their scope of action so much in such a reductive way. Anyway, the structure of this book reminds me of ***Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell*** by Susanna Clarke. Watchmen is even better because multiple media layouts are incorporated into the story. And the philosophical points of this book were well stated and explored. Overall, a must-read in my opinion.
